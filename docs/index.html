Maritime Resilience (PIR 1)

The subsea cable network faces significant physical resilience challenges. The 'Global Undersea Cable Chokepoints and Landing Clusters: A Strategic Risk Assessment' identifies key chokepoints like the Bab-el-Mandeb, Strait of Malacca, and the Strait of Florida. These areas concentrate a large number of cables into narrow geographic locations, making them vulnerable to both accidental damage (anchor drags, fishing) and intentional sabotage, as suggested by the recent Finnish investigation into suspected cable sabotage in the Baltic Sea. A disruption in the Bab-el-Mandeb, for example, could sever Europe-Asia connectivity, impacting nearly $10 trillion in daily financial transactions. Historically, the 2008 Mediterranean cuts and the 2023 Red Sea attacks demonstrated the real-world impact of such vulnerabilities. The concentration of cables increases the potential impact of any single point of failure. The FLAG acquisition of a fiber pair on Google’s Echo Subsea Cable highlights the ongoing commercial interest and investment in these critical routes, but also subtly concentrates control among fewer entities.

Frontier AI Geopolitics (PIR 2)

The strategic placement of new AI/Hyperscale facilities reflects the ongoing 'Great Power Competition,' particularly between the U.S. and China. The 'Global Data Center Density and AI Compute Concentration: Trends and Geopolitical Implications' highlights that the U.S. dominates global hyperscale data center capacity, accounting for over half of the world's total. China is a distant second. The 'Global Evolution of Compute Infrastructure toward Edge' discusses China's massive edge buildout. This suggests a two-pronged approach: the U.S. maintaining a lead in centralized AI compute, while China aggressively expands its distributed edge capabilities. The numerous PLANNED AI builds listed (AWS Sunbury, Google Fort Wayne, xAI Colossus 2) further solidify the U.S. as a primary location for these strategic assets. The existence of planned OpenAI data centers in the UAE also speaks to the extension of this competition into the Middle East. This can be viewed as an expansion of the Digital Silk Road concept, with competing nations trying to establish digital infrastructure and influence globally. The concentration of AI compute power in the hands of a few countries (mostly the US) creates asymmetries in capability as documented in 'Global Data Center Density...'.

Energy & Supply Chain (PIR 3)

Data centers and AI compute facilities are heavily reliant on stable power and cooling infrastructure. These dependencies create potential kinetic vulnerabilities. Power grids and water supplies represent key targets. A successful attack on a major substation or disruption to water supply could cripple large-scale compute facilities. The planned AWS Susquehanna Campus utilizing nuclear power indicates an awareness of the increasing energy demands of these facilities, but also introduces new security considerations. The 'Compute Divide' exacerbates these issues; nations lacking sufficient compute infrastructure are entirely reliant on others, creating a dependency with strategic consequences. It becomes more than just a technological gap, but also a geopolitical vulnerability to those nations. The lack of European firms operating major AI compute hubs highlights this dependency and the risk of being strategically outpaced.

SRM Synthesis & Scenario Modeling

Executive Summary & Scenario Modeling: The global digital infrastructure faces a multi-faceted threat landscape. Physical vulnerabilities at cable chokepoints, coupled with the concentration of AI compute power in a few regions, create significant strategic risks. The US lead in hyperscale compute contrasts with China's edge computing buildout, suggesting different strategic priorities. A successful coordinated cyber-attack on SCADA systems controlling power and cooling could amplify the impact of physical sabotage, leading to cascading failures. Scenario 1 (Cable Sabotage): Coordinated attacks on subsea cables in the Bab-el-Mandeb and Strait of Malacca disrupt global communications and financial transactions. Scenario 2 (AI Compute Disruption): A cyber-attack targeting a major US AI data center cripples key AI services and creates economic and strategic uncertainty. Scenario 3 (Hybrid Warfare): A nation-state combines physical and cyber attacks to simultaneously disrupt cable infrastructure and critical compute facilities, creating widespread chaos and undermining confidence in digital infrastructure. The increasing use of agentic AI may accelerate these attacks, creating novel threat vectors and attack methods.
