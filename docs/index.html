A disruption in any of these locations can have cascading effects on global connectivity. Citing the Global Undersea Cable Chokepoints source, over 95-99% of intercontinental data traffic relies on these cables, with nearly $10 trillion in daily financial transactions dependent on them, emphasizing the criticality and vulnerability of this infrastructure.

Energy & Supply Chain (PIR 3)

Power and cooling constraints are critical vulnerabilities for data centers. The exponential growth in compute demand, driven by AI workloads, is straining existing infrastructure. Generative AI is a prime reason for increased scale - companies are deploying unprecedented clusters of GPUs and accelerators, which demand massive power and space. Nuclear powered and Midwest cloud expansions are being planned in the US to accomodate this growth. Substations and water supply are kinetic vulnerabilities. A disruption to either could cripple data center operations. The Compute Divide, as detailed in Global Data Center Density, exacerbates this issue. Only a small number of countries possess specialized AI data centers, creating a dependency on these nations for AI compute. This concentration of resources makes the supply chain and energy infrastructure supporting these data centers critical targets. Furthermore, this divide limits access to advanced computing for many nations, hindering their ability to develop AI capabilities independently.
