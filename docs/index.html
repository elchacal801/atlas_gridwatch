The increasing sophistication of potential actors and the relative ease of disrupting these cables present a significant and escalating threat.

However, it’s critical to consider that Europe lags significantly. This disparity has implications for technological sovereignty and economic competitiveness. The trend of moving compute to the edge, as detailed in "Global Evolution of Compute Infrastructure toward Edge", introduces a new layer of complexity, as AI applications increasingly rely on distributed infrastructure, potentially shifting the landscape of competition.

Energy & Supply Chain (PIR 3)

Power and cooling constraints represent a significant vulnerability for AI infrastructure. The "Global Data Center Density and AI Compute Concentration: Trends and Geopolitical Implications" report indicates that newer data centers are increasingly power-dense, driven by AI workloads. This demand places strain on energy grids and cooling systems, making these facilities reliant on stable and robust supply chains. Kinetic vulnerabilities, such as substations and water supplies, represent potential single points of failure. A coordinated attack on these critical infrastructures could cripple AI compute capabilities. The 'Compute Divide' exacerbates these vulnerabilities, as nations with limited access to AI infrastructure become even more dependent on a few key providers. This dependence creates strategic vulnerabilities, potentially allowing dominant players to exert undue influence or even restrict access to critical compute resources. Consider the potential impact of energy shortages, component scarcity, or targeted attacks on cooling systems on major AI training hubs.
