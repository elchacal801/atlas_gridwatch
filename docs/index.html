Energy & Supply Chain (PIR 3)

Power and cooling constraints are critical vulnerabilities for AI and hyperscale data centers. These facilities require immense amounts of electricity and advanced cooling systems. Kinetic vulnerabilities include substations and water supply infrastructure, which could be targeted in physical attacks. The "Compute Divide" described in "Global Data Center Density and AI Compute Concentration: Trends and Geopolitical Implications" means that most countries lack the infrastructure for large-scale AI compute, creating a dependence on the few nations that possess it. News regarding Finland's Vatajankoski and E-Heat plugging containerized data centers into local heating networks indicates attempts to mitigate energy consumption and improve efficiency, however these are unlikely to be universally adopted due to geographic and infrastructural limitations. This reliance creates strategic vulnerabilities, as disruptions to these essential resources could cripple AI capabilities. Furthermore, dependencies on specific hardware vendors (e.g., NVIDIA for GPUs) and rare earth minerals exacerbate supply chain risks. Geopolitical tensions could disrupt these supply chains, further impacting AI development and deployment.
