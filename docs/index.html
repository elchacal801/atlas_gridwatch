For example, the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait is a major chokepoint where nearly a dozen major cable systems converge, making it a single point of failure for Europe-Asia connectivity. The Strait of Malacca, English Channel, Strait of Florida, and Isthmus of Panama are also highlighted as areas of concern. Historical precedents such as the 2008 Mediterranean cuts and the 2023 Red Sea attacks demonstrate the impact of cable disruptions. The recent news of the Finnish police investigating suspected cable sabotage further underscores this risk. The concentration of cables makes the network vulnerable to both state and non-state actors seeking to disrupt global communications. The acquisition of fiber pairs by entities like FLAG on Google's Echo subsea cable could lead to increased tension and targeted attacks to disrupt data flow.

The Epoch AI Global Data Center Density analysis reveals a concentration of hyperscale data centers in the US, accounting for over half of the global total, followed by China. This compute asymmetry gives the US a significant edge in AI development and deployment. However, China's rapid edge compute buildout is noteworthy. As the Global Evolution of Compute Infrastructure toward Edge indicates, China has leapfrogged the world in the sheer scale of edge deployment. The planned AI data center buildouts, such as the Adani AI Datacenter in India, and the expansion of US-based AI capabilities (e.g., xAI's Colossus 2 and Memphis Data Center) reflect ongoing competition to dominate the AI landscape. Cuba's infrastructure crisis and reliance on China is enabling China to increase intelligence gathering capabilities against the US with the installation of Chinese telecommunications equipment, giving it access to communication flows. The development of hyperscale facilities globally also impacts the undersea cable network, for example, the FLAG acquisition of the fiber pair on Google's Echo Subsea Cable as well as Japan's increased defense budget and unprecedented number of F-2 Fighters with anti-ship missiles.

Energy & Supply Chain (PIR 3)

Power and cooling constraints are significant vulnerabilities for data centers. Generative AI drives increased scale, demanding massive power and space for GPU clusters as cited in the Global Data Center Density report. The 'Compute Divide' exacerbates this, as most countries lack specialized AI data centers, relying on a few dominant nations. Kinetic vulnerabilities include substations and water supply. A targeted attack on a substation providing power to a major data center could cause significant disruption. The concentration of resources amplifies these risks. For example, the AWS Sunbury and Susquehanna campuses, and the Microsoft Fairwater Wisconsin project, are large targets dependent on reliable energy infrastructure. The Cuba news deep dive reports that the Cuban infrastructure crisis is opening the door to foreign intelligence networks, which is impacting their energy sector.
