Energy & Supply Chain (PIR 3)

Power and cooling are critical constraints for data centers, especially for AI workloads that demand massive computing power. The Global Data Center Density and AI Compute Concentration: Trends and Geopolitical Implications report mentions that newer facilities are increasingly power-dense, often exceeding 100 MW campuses. This places significant strain on local energy grids and cooling infrastructure. Kinetic vulnerabilities, such as substations and water supply systems, can disrupt data center operations. A coordinated attack on these critical infrastructure components could have cascading effects, crippling AI capabilities and cloud services. The 'compute divide' exacerbates this issue, as countries lacking sufficient AI compute infrastructure are more reliant on external providers, increasing their dependence on the resilience of data centers in other nations. This dependence can create strategic vulnerabilities. Furthermore, reliance on specific suppliers for specialized components like GPUs and cooling systems introduces potential bottlenecks and chokepoints in the supply chain. The increasing energy demands also raise concerns about environmental impact and the sustainability of AI development.
