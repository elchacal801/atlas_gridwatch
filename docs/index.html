Energy & Supply Chain (PIR 3)

The operation of modern data centers, especially those supporting AI workloads, is heavily dependent on reliable supply chains for hardware, energy, and cooling. The 'Compute Divide' described in 'Global Data Center Density and AI Compute Concentration' exacerbates vulnerabilities. Most nations lack the infrastructure to support large-scale AI compute, making them reliant on a handful of providers. Power and cooling constraints are major challenges. AI data centers consume vast amounts of electricity, and often require specialized cooling systems. A kinetic attack on critical infrastructure, such as substations or water supply facilities, could cripple a data center and disrupt AI services. For example, the planned AWS Susquehanna Campus's intent of 'Nuclear-Powered Cloud' suggests a direct attempt to mitigate energy dependencies, but also introduces new security considerations. The increasing focus on edge computing, as described in 'Global Evolution of Compute Infrastructure toward Edge', is partly driven by the need to reduce latency and bandwidth demands on core infrastructure, but also highlights the distributed nature of compute resources introducing more areas of vulnerability. The report about a former automotive factory in Wales being eyed for data center development, and Finland’s Vatajankoski plugging a containerized data center into local heating network shows that the energy and environmental dependencies are growing concerns, leading to more creative solutions that introduce additional vulnerabilities.
