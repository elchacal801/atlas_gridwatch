Energy & Supply Chain (PIR 3)

Power and cooling constraints represent significant vulnerabilities for AI and hyperscale facilities. These facilities require massive amounts of electricity to operate, making them heavily reliant on stable and reliable energy grids. The 'Compute Divide,' as mentioned in 'Global Data Center Density and AI Compute Concentration: Trends and Geopolitical Implications,' highlights the concentration of AI compute power in a few countries, which also implies a concentration of energy consumption in those regions. This dependence creates kinetic vulnerabilities, such as substations and water supply systems, that could be targeted to disrupt operations. Planned buildouts like the AWS Susquehanna Campus (nuclear-powered) highlight the increasing focus on securing reliable energy sources for data centers. Cooling systems are equally critical, as AI workloads generate significant heat. Disruptions to water supply, whether through natural disasters or malicious attacks, could cripple cooling infrastructure and force facilities to shut down. The planned Policloud Micro Data Centers and Serverfarm AI Data Centers in France suggest a move towards distributed AI compute, potentially mitigating some of these risks by reducing reliance on centralized facilities. Furthermore, the fact that most nations don't have AI data centers implies that access to compute resources is a concentrated dependency and that these nations will need to secure access to AI resources that are not under their control.
