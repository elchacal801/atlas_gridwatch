Energy & Supply Chain (PIR 3)

Power and cooling constraints represent significant vulnerabilities for data centers and AI infrastructure. The massive power demands of AI workloads, as highlighted in the Global Data Center Density and AI Compute Concentration: Trends and Geopolitical Implications, strain existing energy grids and create dependencies on reliable power sources. The planned AWS Susquehanna Campus in the US, intended to be nuclear-powered, illustrates the growing need for large-scale, sustainable energy solutions. Cooling infrastructure is also crucial, as high-density computing generates significant heat. Disruptions to water supplies or failures in cooling systems can lead to downtime and data loss. SCADA systems controlling substations and water treatment facilities are potential targets for cyberattacks, as discussed below. The 'Compute Divide,' where only a few nations possess the majority of AI compute resources, amplifies these vulnerabilities, as nations lacking indigenous infrastructure become dependent on external providers, creating potential points of coercion or denial of service. Furthermore, the news regarding Vatajankoski and E-Heat's containerized data center plugged into the local heating network highlights some of the innovative solutions being developed to tackle the energy/cooling constraints.
