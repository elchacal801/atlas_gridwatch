Maritime Resilience (PIR 1)

The subsea cable network faces significant physical resilience challenges due to its inherent geography. The "Global Undersea Cable Chokepoints and Landing Clusters: A Strategic Risk Assessment" highlights that over 95-99% of intercontinental data traffic relies on these cables, which converge in narrow passages and coastal landing sites, creating single points of failure. The Red Sea, specifically the Bab-el-Mandeb strait, is a critical chokepoint for Europe-to-Asia cables; disruptions there can sever connectivity, as demonstrated by the 2023 Red Sea attacks. Similar risks exist in the Strait of Malacca and the Strait of Hormuz, and the English Channel due to high cable density. The recent Finnish police investigation into suspected cable sabotage underscores the acute threat. This highlights a recurring vulnerability where single points of failure can create region-wide outages. Redundancy is paramount, but strategic chokepoints naturally limit route diversity.

Frontier AI Geopolitics (PIR 2)

The strategic placement of new AI and hyperscale facilities is deeply intertwined with Great Power Competition, particularly between the US and China. The "Global Data Center Density and AI Compute Concentration" paper details a significant compute divide. The United States dwarfs all other countries in hyperscale infrastructure, which is essential for AI development. The US share of hyperscale sites is widening while Europe's share slips. China has a substantial stake, but the shift from public-sector supercomputers to private AI clusters favors US tech firms. The "Digital Silk Road" initiative, spearheaded by China, aims to counter this imbalance by building digital infrastructure and increasing compute capacity in allied nations. Planned G42 data centers in Vietnam potentially tie into this strategy. The US dominance in AI compute gives it a strategic advantage, but China's efforts to expand its digital footprint, including through investments in edge computing in regions like China, pose a long-term challenge to US hegemony in AI.

Energy & Supply Chain (PIR 3)

Power and cooling represent critical constraints for AI/Hyperscale facilities. These data centers require massive energy consumption, and vulnerabilities at substations or in water supply pose significant kinetic risks. The "Compute Divide" exacerbates this; regions lacking robust infrastructure struggle to compete in AI development. For example, while there are planned builds across the United States, EMEA expansion has no definitive location and has 'unknown' power requirements. The planned AWS Susquehanna Campus, intended to be nuclear-powered, highlights the energy dependency of AI. Cooling is another major constraint; advanced cooling technologies are required to dissipate the heat generated by AI workloads. Disruptions to water supply or cooling systems can cripple data center operations. Supply chain disruptions, whether in semiconductors, cooling equipment, or power infrastructure, pose significant vulnerabilities. The concentration of AI compute in a few regions makes the overall system susceptible to localized failures.
