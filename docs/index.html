Data centers, especially those supporting frontier AI, are energy-intensive and depend on reliable power and cooling infrastructure. The Oak Ridge National Laboratory AI Datacenter Initiative indicates growing concerns about AI datacenter power management. The 'Compute Divide' described in "Global Data Center Density and AI Compute Concentration: Trends and Geopolitical Implications" means that many countries lack the necessary infrastructure and expertise to support advanced AI development, increasing their reliance on a few dominant players. This dependency extends to supply chains for critical components (e.g., GPUs, specialized cooling systems) and resources (e.g., water for cooling). Kinetic vulnerabilities include substations, water treatment plants, and transportation networks essential for delivering these resources. A coordinated attack on these critical nodes could severely disrupt AI capabilities and related services, especially in regions with limited redundancy. The increasing power demands also raise questions about the sustainability of AI and the potential for resource conflicts.
