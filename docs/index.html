Energy & Supply Chain (PIR 3)

The power and cooling requirements for hyperscale data centers and AI training facilities present significant supply chain and energy dependencies. Generative AI models require massive amounts of power and space, necessitating large, power-dense campuses. This creates vulnerabilities related to energy supply, water resources for cooling, and the availability of specialized hardware components. The 'Compute Divide' exacerbates these issues, as the concentration of AI compute power in a few countries makes other nations dependent on these hubs for access to advanced AI capabilities. Kinetic vulnerabilities, such as substations and water supply infrastructure, further compound the risks, as physical attacks on these critical components could disrupt entire data center operations. The news regarding Vatajankoski and E-Heat plugging containerized data centers into local heating networks indicates a potential trend toward more sustainable and resilient energy solutions for data centers, but these remain localized efforts. Projects like the AWS Susquehanna Campus, designed with nuclear power in mind, also highlight the growing demand for reliable and scalable energy sources to power AI workloads.
