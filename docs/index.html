Maritime Resilience (PIR 1)

The physical resilience of the subsea cable network is a critical concern, given its reliance on relatively few physical pathways and geographic chokepoints. As detailed in "Global Undersea Cable Chokepoints and Landing Clusters: A Strategic Risk Assessment," over 95% of intercontinental data traffic relies on these cables, yet they are vulnerable to both accidental and intentional damage. The Bab-el-Mandeb strait, Strait of Malacca, and Strait of Florida are key chokepoints where multiple cables converge, creating single points of failure. Historical precedents, such as the 2008 Mediterranean cuts and the 2023 Red Sea attacks, demonstrate the potential for disruptions to abruptly knock entire regions offline. The recent news of suspected cable sabotage in the Baltic Sea further underscores the ongoing threat to this critical infrastructure. The increasing reliance on these cables for latency-sensitive AI interactions amplifies the potential impact of any disruption.

Planned buildouts, like the Alibaba AI Data Center Expansion, represent China's efforts to close this gap, aligning with its "Digital Silk Road" initiative to expand its digital influence. These trends indicate a geopolitical struggle for AI dominance, manifested in the strategic deployment of compute infrastructure.

Energy & Supply Chain (PIR 3)

The increasing demand for AI compute is straining supply chains and energy resources. "Global Data Center Density and AI Compute Concentration" notes the massive power requirements of AI data centers, necessitating 100+ MW campuses. Cooling is also a critical constraint, with some facilities exploring liquid cooling. Kinetic vulnerabilities include substations and water supply, which could be targeted to disrupt operations. The "Compute Divide" exacerbates these vulnerabilities, as the concentration of AI compute in a few countries (primarily the U.S. and China) leaves most nations without the infrastructure for large-scale AI compute, making them dependent on these leading nations and vulnerable to disruptions in their supply chains and infrastructure.
