Maritime Resilience (PIR 1)

The subsea cable network faces significant physical resilience challenges due to geographic chokepoints. The "Global Undersea Cable Chokepoints and Landing Clusters: A Strategic Risk Assessment" highlights several critical vulnerabilities. The Bab-el-Mandeb strait, a narrow passage between the Horn of Africa and the Arabian Peninsula, concentrates nearly a dozen major cable systems, including SEA-ME-WE 5 and 2Africa. A disruption here could severely impact Europe-Asia connectivity. Similarly, the Strait of Malacca, the primary passage between the Indian Ocean and South China Sea, is another high-risk area due to heavy shipping traffic and shallow channels increasing the risk of anchor drags. The English Channel is also a high-density corridor. Recent news of Finnish authorities investigating suspected cable sabotage in the Baltic Sea underscores the potential for intentional damage, a risk explicitly mentioned in the Knowledge Base. Historically, cable cuts in the Mediterranean in 2008 and the Red Sea attacks in 2023 demonstrate the real-world impact of such events.

The increasing concentration of compute power in the hands of a few nations underscores the asymmetry in AI capability, potentially widening the technological gap between leading and lagging nations.

Energy & Supply Chain (PIR 3)

Power and cooling are critical constraints for AI/Hyperscale facilities. The kinetic vulnerabilities extend to substations and water supply, making these facilities susceptible to physical attacks or natural disasters. The "Compute Divide" further exacerbates this issue. This asymmetry translates into unequal access to AI capabilities and potentially widens the economic and technological gap between nations.
