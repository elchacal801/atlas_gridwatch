Energy & Supply Chain (PIR 3)

Power and cooling constraints represent critical vulnerabilities for AI and hyperscale facilities. This creates dependencies on reliable energy supplies and advanced cooling systems, making these facilities susceptible to disruptions from grid failures or targeted attacks on substations. The 'compute divide' highlighted in "Global Data Center Density and AI Compute Concentration: Trends and Geopolitical Implications", where only a few countries host specialized AI data centers, concentrates these vulnerabilities. Specifically, the planned AWS Susquehanna Campus, intended to be nuclear-powered, exemplifies the increasing reliance on stable, high-capacity energy sources. Kinetic vulnerabilities extend to water supply, essential for cooling, and disruptions to water infrastructure could cripple data center operations. The Finnish example of plugging a containerized data center into the local heating network suggests both an innovation and a mitigation of risk by diversifying heat management.
