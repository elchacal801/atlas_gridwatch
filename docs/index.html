The subsea cable network faces significant physical resilience challenges due to geographic chokepoints. The 'Global Undersea Cable Chokepoints and Landing Clusters' report identifies several critical areas, including the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait, Strait of Malacca, and the Strait of Florida, where numerous cables converge. A disruption in these areas, whether accidental (anchor drag) or intentional (sabotage), can have outsized impacts. For example, the Bab-el-Mandeb is a bottleneck for Europe-to-Asia cables, meaning a single point of failure could sever connectivity. The recent Finnish investigation into suspected cable sabotage highlights this risk. Historical precedents, such as the 2008 Mediterranean cuts and the 2023 Red Sea attacks, demonstrate the vulnerability of these digital lifelines. The concentration of cables in areas like the English Channel and near landing stations further exacerbates the risk, as damage to one cable can easily cascade to others. Increased monitoring of these chokepoints and investment in redundant routes are crucial to mitigating these vulnerabilities.

Energy & Supply Chain (PIR 3)

Power and cooling constraints are increasingly critical for data centers, especially high-density, AI-driven facilities. The article 'Why Effective Facilities Management Is Essential for Today’s Data Centre Operators' notes the unprecedented strain AI compute places on these systems. Kinetic vulnerabilities include substations and water supplies; disruption to either could cripple data center operations. The 'Compute Divide,' described in 'Global Data Center Density and AI Compute Concentration,' exacerbates these vulnerabilities. Countries lacking significant AI compute infrastructure are highly dependent on the US and China, creating a situation where access to advanced AI capabilities could be controlled or limited. This dependency also extends to the supply chain for GPUs and other specialized hardware, where a few key manufacturers dominate. A disruption in this supply chain, whether due to geopolitical tensions or natural disasters, could significantly impact AI development and deployment globally. The recent trend toward edge computing, as noted in 'Global Evolution of Compute Infrastructure toward Edge', seeks to address some of these issues by distributing compute resources closer to end-users, but edge facilities still require reliable power and cooling.
