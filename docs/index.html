The physical resilience of the subsea cable network is a critical vulnerability point. As highlighted in "Global Undersea Cable Chokepoints and Landing Clusters: A Strategic Risk Assessment", over 95% of intercontinental data traffic relies on these cables, which converge at narrow geographic chokepoints like the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait, the Strait of Malacca, and the Strait of Florida. These areas concentrate risk, meaning a single point of failure can disrupt connectivity between entire continents. The recent Finnish investigation into suspected cable sabotage echoes historical precedents such as the 2008 Mediterranean cuts and the 2023 Red Sea attacks, underscoring the ongoing threat to these vital arteries. The concentration of cables in specific landing clusters, such as Egypt for Europe-Asia connectivity, means that disruptions can have an outsized impact. Furthermore, the relative ease with which these cables can be damaged or disrupted (anchor drags, fishing, or deliberate sabotage) amplifies the risk. FLAG's recent acquisition of a fiber pair on Google's Echo subsea cable highlights the ongoing commercial importance of this infrastructure and the potential impact of any disruptions.

Energy & Supply Chain (PIR 3)

Power and cooling constraints are critical factors for data centers, especially those supporting AI workloads. Generative AI requires unprecedented clusters of GPUs and accelerators, demanding massive power and space, as detailed in "Global Data Center Density...". This creates significant energy dependencies, making data centers vulnerable to disruptions in power supply, whether from natural disasters or deliberate attacks. Cooling requirements also pose a challenge, particularly in regions with limited water resources or extreme climates. The "Compute Divide" exacerbates these vulnerabilities, as countries with limited access to advanced computing infrastructure may struggle to develop and deploy AI effectively, further widening the gap between developed and developing nations. Kinetic vulnerabilities, such as substations and water supply infrastructure, represent potential targets for sabotage or disruption. The concentration of AI compute in specific regions (e.g., US) makes these dependencies even more critical.
